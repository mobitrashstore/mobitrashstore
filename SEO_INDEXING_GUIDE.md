# 🚀 Mobi Trash Store: SEO & Indexing Maintenance Guide

Since your website is built with **Static Site Generation (SSG)** via the custom Puppeteer Prerender system, follow this guide to ensure **100% stable indexing** whenever you add new products or blogs.

---

## 📅 When to Re-Index?
You should follow these steps if you:
- Added a batch of new **Products** (e.g. 10-50 New Items).
- Published a new **Blog Post**.
- Changed a **Meta Title** or **Meta Description** for existing products.

---

## 🛠️ Step 1: Re-Generate Static Pages (Local)
On your computer, run this command in your terminal:
```powershell
npm run build:seo
```
**This does three things automatically:**
1.  **Sitemap**: Grabs all new products from Firebase and adds them to `sitemap.xml`.
2.  **Build**: Compiles your React app code.
3.  **Prerender**: Visits EVERY PRODUCT URL invisibly with Puppeteer and saves it as a folder-based `index.html` file in `dist/`.

---

## 📤 Step 2: Upload to cPanel
Once the build is complete:
1.  **Open** the `dist` folder on your computer.
2.  **Upload All** files and folders inside `dist/` to your site's `public_html` folder using File Manager or FTP.
    *   *Make sure you see folders like `/buy/[product_name]/index.html` on your server.*

---

## 📡 Step 3: Google Search Console (GSC)
Google only crawls what it knows about. To speed this up:
1.  **Go to**: [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console)
2.  **Sitemaps**: Ensure `https://mobitrashstore.com/sitemap.xml` is submitted.
3.  **Manual Inspect (Optional)**: If one specific product is urgent, paste its URL into the "URL Inspection" bar and click **"Request Indexing"**.

---

## ❓ FAQ & Troubleshooting

### Why not just wait for Google?
Google *can* crawl your React site without these files, but it is **unstable**. Pre-rendered static files ensure Google sees your **Meta Tags (Title, Description)** and **Content** perfectly on the very first visit.

### What if I edited just 1 product?
If it's just one name change, you don't *have* to rebuild. But it's always best practice to run `npm run build:seo` once a week to keep everything in sync.

### I see ".html" in the old Google results!
Don't worry! We added a redirect in `.htaccess` that automatically sends users and Google from old `.html` links to your new **Clean URLs**. This is called a **301 redirect**, and it passes all your existing "SEO juice" to the new pages.
